## Problem

Conventional recovery of gold from dilute or complex feeds can require chemically intensive
separation, may perform poorly at low concentration, and can generate burdensome residuals.
Biological capture mechanisms may offer molecular selectivity, but direct exposure of living cells
to industrial leachate creates toxicity, containment, variability, and downstream-separation
problems. Meanwhile, urine provides a repeatable, noninvasive source of donor-derived human cells,
but those cells are not known to be a gold-recovery system.

## Core inventive concept

A urine-derived human-cell population is used as a production chassis for an extracellular,
cell-free molecular product having an assayed gold-interaction function. The molecular product is
released from the biological production loop, verified to exclude viable production cells, and
deployed in a physically separate recovery loop. There it contacts a feed containing pre-existing
ionic gold, selectively captures the gold, and supports or precedes conversion into a recoverable
gold-containing solid. The living donor-derived cells never contact the industrial feed.

## Potentially distinguishing combination

The candidate inventive combination is not merely “cells plus gold.” It includes:

1. a noninvasively sourced urine-derived human-cell production platform;
2. manufacture of a defined extracellular affinity or redox-active product;
3. release of that product as a cell-free intermediate;
4. physical separation of the biological and metal-processing loops;
5. selective capture of pre-existing dissolved gold from a characterized mixed-ion feed;
6. optional independent capture and reduction/mineralization stages;
7. recovery, regeneration, or immobilization of the active molecular fraction;
8. closed, stage-resolved gold mass balance and lot traceability;
9. explicit exclusion of biological elemental transmutation.

Each element may exist separately in prior work. Novelty and nonobviousness of the combination must
be established through a professional search and claim analysis.

## System embodiments

### Embodiment A — soluble secretome capture

A released soluble fraction contacts the feed in a closed module. Gold-loaded molecules are
separated by a property orthogonal to gold binding. Gold is then released or converted to a
recoverable solid, and the molecular fraction is recycled when stability permits.

### Embodiment B — immobilized affinity phase

The active extracellular component is immobilized on a contained support. Feed passes through the
support until a predetermined breakthrough criterion. The loaded support is transferred to a
separate recovery step and regenerated or retired under a validated waste plan.

### Embodiment C — vesicle-displayed affinity

Released extracellular vesicles present a repeated gold-interacting surface. A physical process
captures the loaded vesicles without permitting viable cells into the recovery loop. Vesicles are
single-use or reusable according to measured retention.

### Embodiment D — decoupled capture and mineralization

One molecular component provides selectivity for ionic gold; a second cell-free component or
controlled chemical stage changes oxidation state and produces a separable gold-containing solid.
This embodiment makes selectivity and conversion independently controllable.

### Embodiment E — modular cell chassis

Urine-derived primary cells, progenitor cells, reprogrammed cells, differentiated secretory cells,
or an established derivative cell bank manufacture the extracellular product. The claim value of
each chassis depends on experimental support and prior art.

## Method scaffold for counsel

A candidate independent method claim could be organized around:

1. obtaining or maintaining a human cell population derived from a urine specimen;
2. causing the population to produce an extracellular molecular composition having a measured
   affinity for an ionic gold species;
3. separating viable cells from the extracellular composition;
4. verifying the composition satisfies a cell-exclusion release criterion;
5. contacting the released composition with a nonbiological feed containing pre-existing ionic
   gold in a physically separate process loop;
6. recovering gold associated with the extracellular composition; and
7. producing and assaying a gold-containing solid while closing the material balance.

Candidate dependent limitations include immobilization, extracellular-vesicle display, a defined
selectivity factor, two-stage capture/reduction, capacity-based breakthrough control, regeneration,
lot-level traceability, and independently assayed mixed-ion feed.

## System scaffold for counsel

A candidate system claim could recite:

- a contained urine-derived-cell production module;
- a viable-cell exclusion and product-release module;
- a separate feed-characterization module;
- a cell-free affinity-capture module fluidically isolated from the production module;
- a gold conversion/mineralization module;
- a solid-liquid separation module;
- sensors and records sufficient to calculate stage-resolved gold mass balance; and
- an interlock preventing unreleased biological product or unqualified feed from entering capture.
