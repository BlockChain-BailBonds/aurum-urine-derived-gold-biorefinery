# Scientific basis

## 1. Atomic boundary

Gold is defined by a nucleus containing 79 protons. Cell metabolism operates primarily through
chemical reactions involving electrons, molecular bonds, ions, and redox state. It cannot add or
remove enough nuclear protons to turn another element into gold. Elemental transmutation requires
a nuclear process and is outside AURUM's scope.

AURUM therefore uses the words **recover**, **capture**, **reduce**, **precipitate**, and
**biomineralize**. It does not use **create gold** as a material claim.

## 2. Urine-derived cell precedent

Voided urine contains exfoliated human cells, including clonogenic populations described as
urine-derived stem or progenitor cells. Literature reports noninvasive collection, expansion,
reprogramming, and differentiation into several human cell lineages. This establishes urine as a
possible donor-cell source.

It does not establish that:

- urine-derived cells can become bacteria, fungi, or independent nonhuman organisms;
- they naturally express a high-capacity gold metallophore;
- their secretions remain active in industrial leachate;
- urine itself contains enough gold for commercial recovery.

The AURUM architecture treats the cells as a manufacturing chassis for a candidate molecular
product, not as a gold source and not as an alchemical transmutation system.

## 3. Biological gold-cycling precedent

Published microbial work supports multiple mechanisms relevant to a recovery system:

- resistance and detoxification of soluble Au complexes;
- reduction of ionic gold species to elemental particles;
- secretion of a gold-interacting metallophore, delftibactin;
- fungal mediation of gold oxidation, complexation, transport, and redistribution.

These mechanisms demonstrate that living systems can change gold's chemical form and location.
They do not demonstrate gold synthesis from non-gold atoms.

## 4. The translational gap

The project's central leap is transferring a useful extracellular capture function into, or
deriving an equivalent function from, a urine-derived human-cell production platform. That leap
has at least five independent unknowns:

1. whether the platform can produce enough active material;
2. whether the product binds ionic gold with useful affinity and capacity;
3. whether competing ions overwhelm selectivity;
4. whether binding can be coupled to controlled Au(0) formation;
5. whether the product is stable, reusable, and economical outside cell-culture conditions.

Each unknown has a matched experiment and failure gate in `VALIDATION_PLAN.md`.

## 5. Candidate molecular modes

Candidate mechanisms are design options, not validated selections:

| Mode | Intended function | Principal uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Secreted peptide or protein | Coordinate Au(I)/Au(III) selectively | Capacity and competing-ion selectivity |
| Extracellular vesicle display | Present repeated affinity motifs | Isolation cost and product heterogeneity |
| Immobilized secretome fraction | Create reusable capture surface | Activity after immobilization and regeneration |
| Enzymatic or redox-active fraction | Convert captured Au ions toward Au(0) | Specificity and byproducts |
| Two-component capture/reduction | Separate selectivity from mineralization | Added unit operations and cost |

The preferred engineering strategy is to decouple capture from reduction. It makes each stage
measurable, prevents apparent particle formation from being mistaken for selective recovery, and
allows independent optimization.
