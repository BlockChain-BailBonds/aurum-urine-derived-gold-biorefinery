# Safety, ethics, and containment

## Non-protocol status

This repository is an engineering and computational research specification. It does not authorize
or provide a home procedure for culturing human specimens, modifying cells, processing e-waste,
handling gold salts, generating nanoparticles, or refining metal.

## Human-origin material

Urine is a human biospecimen and must be handled under an institutionally approved exposure-control
and biosafety program. “Noninvasive collection” does not mean risk-free or ethically unrestricted.
Work must address:

- informed consent and the intended commercial/research use;
- the possibility of future cell-line engineering and long-term storage;
- withdrawal limits once material has been transformed or distributed;
- coded identifiers, restricted re-identification keys, and retention schedules;
- incidental findings and whether they will be generated or returned;
- donor compensation, benefit sharing, and noncoercive recruitment;
- destruction, transfer, and secondary-use rules.

Collection directly from living people for a systematic investigation may constitute human-subject
research even when the investigator later codes the sample. The responsible institution—not an
individual investigator—must determine IRB or ethics-review requirements.

## Genetic engineering

Any use of recombinant or synthetic nucleic acids requires a project-specific applicability and
containment determination. Where applicable, institutional biosafety committee review, trained
personnel, incident reporting, and approved emergency plans precede work. This repository contains
no genetic construct, sequence, vector, transfection recipe, or release design.

## Chemical and nanoparticle hazards

Gold-bearing feeds may contain corrosive agents, toxic metals, complexing ligands, or unknown
contaminants. Nanoscale gold can have exposure and environmental behavior different from bulk
metal. Required controls are determined from a process hazard analysis and include, as applicable:

- closed transfer and secondary containment;
- ventilation and exposure monitoring;
- compatible materials and pressure/temperature limits;
- chemical segregation and verified labeling;
- spill, fire, exposure, and loss-of-containment procedures;
- effluent characterization before any recycle or discharge;
- documented hazardous-waste determinations and licensed disposal.

## Two-loop containment

The cell-production loop and industrial-feed loop must remain physically separate. The interface
is a qualified cell-free capture product. This prevents:

- viable donor-derived cells from entering industrial waste;
- toxic feed from reaching the cell culture;
- uncontrolled biological adaptation in a metal-rich process environment;
- donor material from contaminating the recovered product;
- process contaminants from entering the cell bank.

No viable engineered cells are intentionally released. A cell-exclusion failure quarantines the
entire secretome lot and any connected recovery material.

## Claims ethics

The following are prohibited project representations:

- that human cells transmute other elements into gold;
- that donating urine is a medical treatment or guaranteed income source;
- that modeled revenue is demonstrated revenue;
- that “biological” means nontoxic, sustainable, or safe;
- that a donor has ownership or royalty rights not established in written agreements;
- that a favorable result has been replicated when it has not.
